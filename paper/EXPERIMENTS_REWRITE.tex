\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments answer three questions in turn. Can gaze on a
persistent instance map single out one of several identical,
unmarked objects, and where does it fail (E1)? Which mechanisms of
Sec.~\ref{sec:method} carry the $1^\circ$ budget, measured by
switching each one off in bit-exact replay (E4)? Does a resolved
referent survive the trip to a robot that may never have seen it
(E2, E3)?

\subsection{Setup and Protocol}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\textbf{Platform.} A Pupil Core eye tracker; a Unitree B2 quadruped
with an arm; an iPhone 16 Pro Max for mapping; an RTX 4090
workstation for reconstruction and replay. Speech is transcribed
with faster-whisper (word timestamps) and parsed by GPT-5 mini into
an action with object/place/destination slots (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).

\textbf{Maps.} One room, four independently arranged and mapped
layouts (78--144 photos each; the two most recent maps hold 259 and 64
instances, 13 and 11 of them named). Named objects include three
identical tennis balls, two identical white cups, and several
everyday items placed within a few centimeters of one another. No
object carries a marker.

\textbf{Wearers and calibration.} Three wearers; the tracker is
recalibrated after every donning, with a measured accuracy of
$0.8$--$1.1^\circ$. The cone width is fixed at $\sigma=1^\circ$ for
every result below; E4 sweeps it.

\textbf{Fixation protocol.} Each recording follows a pre-registered
card: a sequence of 12--15 named targets, each fixated for 2--3\,s
from a fixed standing position (\TBD{n-stations} stations,
$1.0$--$4.6$\,m from the objects). For every station--target pair we
compute $\theta$, the minimum angular separation between the target
and any other named instance, from the median head pose and the map
geometry, independently of the outcome
($0.1^\circ\le\theta\le24^\circ$). $\theta$ is the single axis on
which Sec.~\ref{sec:method} predicts failure. Four further recordings
are taken while walking laterally at $1$--$3$\,m.

\textbf{Scoring.} Recordings are replayed offline through the frozen
resolver. Outputs naming the same instance within $1.2$\,s are
merged; merged segments shorter than $1.0$\,s are dropped; within each
target's time slot the instance with the longest accumulated
fixation is the prediction. A target counts as correct only if the
predicted instance is correct in sequence; unmatched outputs are
counted separately as extra bindings.

\textbf{Data hygiene.} All 330 static trials recorded under the
frozen configuration are reported, except three in which the wearer
fixated a different ball than instructed (the system bound the
fixated ball); these are excluded as execution errors, leaving 327.
Two earlier recordings (30 trials) were dropped before scoring for
instrumentation faults, not outcomes: a localization dead zone (95 of
3833 frames localized) and a cart displaced $5.8$\,cm after mapping.
Two pre-registered choices were revised before the final runs and are
reported as run: the table prior was set to zero, and the planned
re-layout batch was replaced by the four independently mapped
layouts. The first author performed 90\% of gaze trials; the other
two wearers contributed 30 and 12 trials after their own calibration.

\textbf{Robot trials.} 39 end-to-end trials: 20 with full session
logs and 19 scored on site. A trial succeeds only if the robot
reaches the correct instance, grasps it, and completes the requested
handover or placement without intervention. Eight target-matched
pairs compare the robot starting in view of the target with the robot
starting outside the room.

\subsection{E1: Accuracy versus Angular Separation}
\label{sec:disambiguation_results}

Fig.~\ref{fig:theta} plots top-1 binding accuracy against $\theta$
for the 327 static trials. Three regimes appear. For
$\theta\ge2.5^\circ$ the resolver binds 151 of 154 targets
($98.1\%$, Wilson 95\% CI $[94.4, 99.4]$). Between $1^\circ$ and
$2.5^\circ$ --- one to two calibrated $\sigma$ --- it binds 77 of 88
($87.5\%$): the knee sits where the cone width predicts. Below
$1^\circ$ the outcome depends on whether the target is physically
visible: with a clear sightline the resolver still binds 26 of 37
($70.3\%$, $[54.2, 82.5]$), whereas targets hidden behind a nearer
object --- balls in a row seen obliquely past the $4$\,m limit, or a
second-row item behind a front one at $3$\,m --- fall to 16 of 48
($33.3\%$, $[21.7, 47.5]$), the three-candidate chance level. The
collapse is therefore a visibility limit of the scene, not a
precision limit of the gaze channel.

Walking does not break the world-frame fixation events: 56 of 57
targets bind correctly across four lateral-walking recordings
($\theta$ mostly $1.3$--$2.8^\circ$). The two additional wearers bind
15/15 and 12/12 of their trials.

\subsection{E2, E3: End-to-End Retrieval}
\label{sec:end_to_end_results}

Fig.~\ref{fig:story} shows the two task forms: fetch-and-handover
(``Pick this up'' / ``Hand it to me'') and fetch-and-place (``Pick up
this apple and put it here''), the destination given by a second
fixation. In both, the measured gaze lands on the tabletop beside the
fruit; the visible-surface vote still assigns it to the apple
instance, whose identity the robot then re-acquires at the table.

The robot completed 36 of 39 trials ($92\%$): 28/30 with a deictic
reference resolved by gaze, 8/9 by category name, and 2/2 placements.
The three failures are, in the taxonomy of Sec.~\ref{sec:method}:
twice the wearer fixated a neighboring instance (the robot fetched
what was looked at), once no binding cleared the vote threshold and
the system refused. Every trial on the pair of identical white cups
selected the correct cup. In the eight target-matched pairs the robot
succeeded 7/8 with the target in view and 7/8 starting outside the
room: resolution is settled in the map before the robot moves, and
entering the room is only navigation. Median execution time from
accepted order to delivery was $5.4$\,s ($n{=}14$, IQR $5.3$--$7.4$);
speech end to dispatch took $2.2$\,s with a cached parse and $5.0$\,s
with a cold LLM call, of which transcription is $\sim$$1.5$\,s.
Emergency stop measured \TBD{sys-estop}.

\subsection{E4: Replay Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation_results}

Every session replays bit-exactly, so each mechanism can be switched
off on the same recordings. Table~\ref{tab:ablations} does this on the
204-trial frozen subset used for method freezing (bins $n=58/66/78$,
walking $n=15$); each row removes one mechanism and restores the naive
design it replaced.

Three mechanisms carry the result. Collapsing the cone to a near
single ray loses a third of all bindings and almost every walking
trial: a fixation is a region, and evidence off the central ray is
what recovers the target when the ray misses. Voting over a sphere
around the gaze point instead of over rendered visible surfaces
loses most of the sub-$\sigma$ bin (50 to 15 of 78): invisible
surfaces behind or beneath the target outvote it exactly where
neighbors are close. Letting every instance --- or the tabletop ---
compete for votes collapses accuracy (39 and 144 of 204) and
destroys walking (2 of 15), because unnamed fragments and supporting
surfaces sweep through the cone continuously. The remaining
mechanisms are refinements: angular clustering adds seven sub-$\sigma$
bindings over a fixed metric radius, area normalization trades one
hit for fewer extra bindings, and the interactability prior matters
only in the hard bin and while walking.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma} varies $\sigma$ alone. Correct bindings in the
$1$--$2.5^\circ$ bin are 29, 42, 58, 58, 47, and 26 of 66 for
$\sigma=0.2, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2.5, 4^\circ$ (all trials: 102, 131, 166,
152, 130, 92 of 204; sub-$\sigma$ tier: 23, 34, 50, 38, 27, 15 of 78).
The optimum coincides with the calibrated
$0.8$--$1.1^\circ$: narrower cones miss surfaces displaced from the
central ray, wider cones admit the neighbors the task is meant to
separate.
